## What do you own and what do you rent online?

Most Gold Coast tradies have their business spread across four or five places online, and only some of them are truly theirs. The simplest test is to ask what you would still have if a platform shut your account tomorrow.

Facebook, Instagram and your Google Business Profile are rented. The platform sets the rules, decides who sees your posts and can restrict or remove your account.

Your domain name, your website content and your customer list are owned. You can move them to a new host, a new designer or a new email tool without starting again.

Knowing which is which tells you where to put the work that has to last.

*What a typical trade business owns versus rents online*

| Asset | Owned or rented | Who sets the rules | What you keep if it goes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Facebook Page | Rented | Meta | Only the photos and posts you saved yourself |
| Instagram profile | Rented | Meta | Only the photos and posts you saved yourself |
| Google Business Profile | Rented | Google | Only the photos and details you saved yourself |
| Domain name | Owned while it stays registered and renewed in your name | You, under your registrar's terms | The address your customers already know |
| Website content | Owned, as long as you keep a copy | You | Pages, photos and text you can move to a new host |
| Business email on your domain | Owned address | You | The same email address, even if you change email provider |
| Customer email and phone list | Owned | You | Every contact, ready for your next message about repeat work |

## What happens to your followers if your page is hacked or disabled?

If your Facebook page is hacked or disabled, your followers, posts and message history sit behind that lock until Meta restores your access. You cannot message those followers anywhere else in the meantime.

The same ABS release found 21% of Australian businesses had a cyber security incident in 2024-25. That figure covers every type of incident, not just Facebook takeovers, but it shows this is not a rare event for Australian businesses.

It also found 28% of businesses had no cyber security prevention measures in place. If your page relies on one personal login with no two-step verification, treat yourself as part of that group until you fix it.

With a website and your own customer list in place, a locked page costs you some posts rather than your whole pipeline.

## Can you get your followers' contact details out of Facebook?

No, following your page does not hand you anyone's phone number or email address. Your follower count is a list Meta holds, and you can only reach those people through Facebook, on Facebook's terms.

Check Meta's own Help Centre for what you can currently download from your Page. We have not listed it here because it changes, and we only quote primary sources we have checked.

The fix is to collect contact details at the job rather than on the platform. Every quote, invoice and booking is a chance to add a name, number and email to a list that belongs to you.

Do that for a year and you will have a list you can contact for repeat work, whatever happens to your page.

## Who owns your Facebook Page, and how do you recover a disabled one?

Whoever holds full admin control of your Page has the power to run it, change it or remove you from it. That is a real risk if the only admin is a former employee, an ex-partner or the relative who set it up years ago.

If your Page is disabled, the appeal goes through Meta, and the steps change often enough that any blog post can go out of date. Start at Meta's own Help Centre and follow the current process for your situation.

We will add links to Meta's own enforcement and appeal documentation once we have checked it against the live pages. Until then, treat Meta's Help Centre as the only authority on recovery.

Sorting out your admin roles today is the simplest insurance you can buy for the page you have built.

## What should you set up before something goes wrong?

Spend half an hour on these steps before you ever need them. Each one protects the work you have already put into your page.

- Add a second trusted admin to your Page, so someone else in the business can still get in if you are locked out.
- Turn on two-factor authentication for every admin, so a stolen password alone is not enough to take over your page.
- Remove admin access for anyone who no longer works with you, because an old login is an open door you do not need.
- Save copies of your best job photos outside Facebook, in a phone folder or cloud storage, so you can rebuild anywhere if you have to.
- Collect a phone number and email from every customer into your own list, because that list is the one part of your marketing no platform can switch off.
- Put your website address and phone number in your Page details and your pinned post, so customers can still find you if the page ever goes quiet.

## When is Facebook alone genuinely fine?

Facebook alone is fine when you are booked out on referrals, most of your work comes from people who already know you, and you are not trying to win jobs from strangers.

Plenty of good tradies across SEQ run their businesses this way. If the last ten customers test backs that up, a website can wait while you collect customer contacts.

Watch for these signs that it is time to build one.

- Customers ask whether you have a website before they book, which tells you they want more proof than a Facebook page gives them.
- You want work in suburbs where nobody knows your name yet, and referrals cannot reach people who have never heard of you.
- You are missing Facebook messages while you are on the tools, and a website with a phone number and enquiry form gives customers another way to reach you.
- You want to run ads and need somewhere to send people that is built around your services rather than a busy newsfeed.
- Your enquiries drop whenever Facebook changes what it shows, which means too much of your pipeline depends on one platform.
- If two or more of these sound familiar, it is time to give customers a place you own to land.

A Google Business Profile is one of the most useful free listings a local tradie can have, but it is rented from Google too, and it works best with a website behind it.

Your profile is what shows on Google Maps when someone in Southport searches for an electrician. A website gives that profile somewhere to send people for your full services, areas, photos and proof.

## What do ChatGPT and AI answers read?

AI answer tools can only recommend you from information they can find and read, and your own website is the one place where you decide what that information says.

Our SEO work starts from Google's own documentation, which says a page needs to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Google lists no extra requirements beyond that.

For local details such as your hours, service area and services, we treat your Google Business Profile as the route Google documents for business information reaching its AI answers.

We have not found a primary source from Meta or OpenAI that sets out how well ChatGPT-style tools can read a Facebook page, so we do not build anyone's plan on it. We go further into what is documented in [how to get recommended by ChatGPT](/insights/how-to-get-recommended-by-chatgpt).

## How do you connect Facebook and your website?

Connect your channels so each one feeds the others, with your website at the centre. If you are short on things to share, [one job can become five posts](/insights/what-to-post-on-social-media-for-small-business) that all point back to your site.

- Add your website address to your Facebook Page details and your Instagram bio, so anyone checking you out can go straight to your services and contact details.
- Pin a post that links to your website, so new visitors to your page see where to find the full detail on your work.
- Link your website from your Google Business Profile, so Google searchers have a direct path to your services and areas.
- Share each finished job on Facebook with a link to the matching service page, so the post sends people to a page that keeps working after the post scrolls away.
- Add a simple enquiry form or email sign-up to your website, so each enquiry adds to the customer list you own.

## What about Instagram, TikTok and Linktree?

Instagram and TikTok are rented in exactly the same way as Facebook, so the same rule applies: build an audience there, and keep your customer contacts and your main business details somewhere you own.

A Linktree-style link page sits on someone else's platform as well. It is a handy signpost in a bio, but it cannot hold your customer list or explain your services the way your own site can.

Treat every social profile as a doorway to your website, and your followers on any platform have a way to become customers you can contact directly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a Facebook page enough for a tradie?**
It can be, if most of your last ten customers came from word of mouth and repeat work. If any came from Google or from suburbs where nobody knew you, a website and a Google Business Profile will catch work your Facebook page misses.

**Is Facebook a website?**
No, a Facebook page is a profile on Meta's platform, and Meta sets the rules for who sees it and whether it stays up. A website sits on your own domain, and you decide what it says and where it is hosted.

**Can I get my followers' phone numbers or emails from Facebook?**
No, following your page does not give you anyone's contact details. Collect names, numbers and emails at each quote, invoice or booking instead, so you build a customer list you own.

**What happens if my Facebook page gets hacked?**
You lose access to your followers, posts and messages until Meta restores it, so set up a second admin and two-factor authentication now. The ABS found 21% of Australian businesses had a cyber security incident of some kind in 2024-25, and 28% had no prevention measures in place.

**Is a Google Business Profile enough without a website?**
It is a strong start, but it is rented from Google too. Your profile works best when it links to a website that shows your services, your areas and real photos of your jobs.
