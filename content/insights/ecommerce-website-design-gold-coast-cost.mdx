An ecommerce website on the Gold Coast costs anywhere from $5k to $80k+ to build, going by the tiers local agencies publish. The price depends on how many products you sell, how much custom work you need and what the quote leaves out.

We plan and build [ecommerce websites for Gold Coast businesses](/websites), and this guide puts the build cost, the running costs, the GST and ACCC rules and the gaps in a typical quote on one page. - Published Gold Coast agency ranges for an ecommerce build run from $5k, the bottom of Talk Digital's Essential tier, to $80k+, the top of its Scale tier, with WP Creative at $10,000 to $50,000+ and Titan Blue at $8,000 to $25,000+.

- Titan Blue advises adding 20 to 40 per cent to a quote if you cannot supply copy and photography, a figure it publishes without independent verification.

- The ACCC says online businesses carry the same consumer guarantee obligations as physical shops, and it recommends showing clear refund steps, a phone number, a physical address and an ABN.
- Many trade businesses need online booking, quote requests or deposits rather than a full store, which means a smaller build and fewer running costs.

## What does each budget tier actually buy?

Each agency defines its own inclusions, so read the fine print on any page you compare against. The descriptions below are our general guide to what each band of the market usually covers, using Talk Digital's tier names as the reference points.

The higher the tier, the more of the store is designed and built around your business rather than adapted from a template.

- An Essential store ($5k to $12k) usually runs on a ready-made theme with light styling, a standard checkout, a modest product range and the core payment and shipping settings switched on.
- A Growth store ($12k to $30k) usually adds a custom design, more products and collections, integrations with tools such as your accounting or email software, and more care with search set-up and page speed.
- A Scale store ($30k to $80k+) usually covers large catalogues, complex product options, custom integrations with inventory or ordering systems, wholesale pricing or selling into more than one market.

## Shopify or WooCommerce: how do the costs differ?

Shopify and WooCommerce both sit inside the published ranges, but they charge you in different ways. Shopify is a hosted platform you subscribe to, while WooCommerce is a free plugin that turns a WordPress site into a store you host and maintain yourself.

That difference shifts cost from the build into the running budget. Shopify bundles hosting and platform updates into its plan, while WooCommerce asks you to pay for hosting, security and someone to keep WordPress and its plugins up to date.

For most small Gold Coast businesses with a modest catalogue and no developer on staff, we lean towards Shopify because there is less to maintain. WooCommerce suits businesses already running a WordPress site who want their content and store in one place.

When Trigahex, a colour-matching card game made in Queensland, needed a store built to sell, we moved it from Wix to Shopify and rebuilt the homepage and product page. She added: "I started with a sales goal to reach within 6 months and we smashed those numbers in just a few weeks." You can see more of [our client case studies](/work).

Choosing the platform that matches how hands-on you want to be keeps your running costs predictable.

## How do you work out the year-one cost?

Add the build fee to a full year of running costs, and you get the number that matters. A store that is cheaper to build can easily cost more to own if it relies on several paid apps or needs regular developer time.

Use this list to build your own year-one figure before you compare quotes.

- Start with the build fee from the quote, and check whether it covers design, development, set-up and training.
- Add any content costs, such as photography, copywriting and product data entry, if you are not supplying them yourself.
- Add a full year of the platform plan or hosting, plus every paid app or extension the build depends on.
- Estimate payment fees as a share of your expected sales, including any buy now, pay later fees.
- Add your domain, email and a full year of maintenance or support.
- Set aside a marketing budget, because a store with no traffic makes no sales.

## What costs extra on top of the quote?

Most build quotes cover design and development, and the costs that blow out a budget usually sit outside them. Titan Blue says to add 20 to 40 per cent to a quote if you cannot supply copy and photography, though that figure is its own and has not been independently verified.

If you plan to write your own product pages, our guide to [writing great website copy](/insights/writing-great-website-copy) will help you get them done before the build starts.

Asking which of these items are included lets you compare quotes like for like.

- Product photography is often excluded, and good photos of real products do more to sell than stock images.
- Copywriting for product, category and policy pages is often quoted separately or left to you.
- Product data entry, including titles, prices, variants, weights and dimensions, takes real hours on a large catalogue.
- Migration from an old store, such as moving products, customers and order history, is usually priced on its own.
- Integrations with accounting, inventory or booking software can add significant development time.
- Shipping rules, tax settings and training are sometimes treated as extras rather than part of the build.

## How long does an ecommerce build take, and what slows it down?

Gold Coast agencies quote 6 to 12 weeks (WP Creative) and 8 to 16 weeks (Titan Blue and Creative Ground) for an ecommerce build. The difference usually comes down to scope and how ready the business is when work begins.

Late product data, missing photos, slow feedback rounds and third-party integrations are the usual reasons a launch slips. Payment provider approval and shipping account set-up can also add time if they start late.

Having your products, photos and decisions ready on day one is the fastest way to a launch date you can plan around.

## When does DIY stop being cheaper than an agency?

Building your own store on Shopify is cheaper in cash, and it can make sense for a handful of products with simple shipping. It stops being cheaper when the hours you spend learning the platform come out of paid work on the tools.

DIY also gets harder once you need integrations, custom product options, migration from an old store or search set-up done properly. Those jobs are where a small mistake costs sales for months.

Be honest about your time, and you will know quickly which path costs less.

## Does a trade business need an online store at all?

Many Gold Coast trade businesses do not need a full ecommerce store, because their customers book a job rather than buy a product. A plumber, electrician or landscaper usually gets more from online booking, quote requests with photo uploads or a deposit payment than from a shopping cart.

A store makes sense when you genuinely sell products, such as a landscaper selling plants and supplies, or when you sell gift vouchers or fixed-price service packages. We compare all three paths in [ecommerce or simple website for Gold Coast trade businesses](/insights/ecommerce-website-design-gold-coast).

Choosing the smallest option that does the job saves you both build cost and running costs.

## Which payment and buy-now-pay-later options work in Australia?

Australian stores commonly take card payments through Shopify Payments, Stripe, Square or PayPal, and offer buy now, pay later through Afterpay or Zip. Each provider charges its own fees, so compare current rates directly with them.

Offering the payment methods your customers already use removes a reason to abandon the checkout.

## How do shipping rules work with Australia Post and Sendle?

Shipping in an online store runs on rules you set, such as flat rates, free shipping over a set order value or live rates pulled from Australia Post or Sendle. Live rates only work if every product has an accurate weight and dimensions.

Gold Coast businesses can also offer local delivery or click and collect for nearby customers. Getting shipping right before launch stops you losing margin on every heavy parcel.

## Do you need to register for GST to sell online?

You must register for GST once your GST turnover reaches $75,000 or more, according to the [ATO's guide to registering for GST](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/registering-for-gst). You have 21 days from reaching the threshold to register.

The ATO measures GST turnover two ways: current turnover, which is this month plus the previous 11 months, and projected turnover, which is this month plus the next 11 months. A strong launch can push your projected turnover over the line sooner than you expect.

If you were required to register and did not, the ATO says you may have to pay GST on sales since that date, even if GST was not in your price, and penalties and interest may apply. Talk to your accountant about how prices should display and set your checkout to calculate GST correctly from day one.

Sorting GST before launch protects your margin from a backdated bill.

## What must your store show for refunds and consumer guarantees?

Online businesses have the same responsibilities to consumers as physical businesses, according to the [ACCC's guidance on buying online](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/buying-online). That means following the consumer guarantees and not making false or misleading claims on your website, on social media or in how you handle reviews.

The ACCC recommends that sellers display clear refund steps, a phone number, a physical address and an ABN. If you collect reviews on your store, our guide to [asking customers for Google reviews within the ACCC rules](/insights/how-to-ask-customers-for-google-reviews) covers what is allowed.

Clear contact details and refund steps build the trust a first-time buyer needs to check out.

## What privacy and security obligations apply?

Your store collects names, addresses and order details, so it needs a privacy policy that says what you collect and how you use it. Check with the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner about whether the Privacy Act applies to your business.

Use a hosted checkout from your payment provider so card details never sit on your own server. Turn on two-factor login for every admin account, and remove access for anyone who no longer needs it.

Good security habits protect your customers and keep your store open for business.

## How do you judge a quote, and what are the red flags?

A good ecommerce quote is itemised, names the platform and lists what is and is not included. Some ranking pages state conversion or AI search figures with no source you can check, and we leave those out of this guide.

Spotting these red flags early saves you from a store that costs more than it should.

- A single price with no breakdown makes it impossible to compare quotes or see what is missing.
- A quote that is silent on content, product data entry and migration usually means those costs arrive later.
- A quote with no running costs listed hides part of the real price of the store.
- An agency that registers the domain or platform account in its own name can make it hard for you to leave.
- Bold claims about sales or AI rankings without a source you can open are a reason to ask more questions.

## Should you hire a local agency or go offshore?

A local Gold Coast agency works in your time zone, can meet you in person and understands Australian payments, shipping, GST and consumer law. An offshore team can quote lower, but you take on more of the project management and the checking yourself.

Whoever you choose, ask who does the work, who you talk to and who supports the store after launch. Clear answers to those questions matter more than the postcode on the invoice.

## Who owns the store and the domain when the build is done?

You should own the domain, the Shopify store or hosting account, and full admin access to everything, all registered in your business name. Ask for this in writing before you sign.

Check whether the theme licence and paid apps transfer to you, and who holds the login details. Owning your store outright means you can change agencies without starting again.
