SEO usually takes three to six months to produce measurable results, and six to 12 months in competitive industries for significant ranking and revenue impact, according to [Search Engine Land's guide to SEO timelines](https://searchengineland.com/guide/how-long-does-seo-take-to-work). If you run a trade on the Gold Coast or anywhere in South East Queensland, that is the honest planning window for [SEO and search work](/search), and this guide shows you what should happen inside it.

I'll separate the figures that come from Google from the ones that come from polls, show you what to check at each stage, and explain how ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews fit into the same picture. You'll finish knowing when to be patient and when to start asking questions.

## Key Points

- Search Engine Land puts measurable SEO results at three to six months, and six to 12 months in competitive industries for significant ranking and revenue impact.
- Google's John Mueller has said it could take up to a year for Google to figure out where to rank a new website.
- The widely quoted three-to-six-month figure from Ahrefs comes from polls of SEO practitioners on LinkedIn and X, which makes it opinion data rather than a controlled study.
- Google's own documentation says some SEO changes can take effect in a few days, while confirming a whole site is helpful and reliable can take several months.
- A Google patent on rank transition, as cited in Search Engine Land's guide, describes a page's rank dipping for about 20 days and settling into its new steady-state value roughly 70 days after a positive link-based change.
- In our experience, AI answers tend to draw on pages that are already indexed and ranking, so there is no separate shortcut into ChatGPT or AI Overviews.

## Why does SEO take so long?

Google has to find your changes, crawl them, process them and then decide whether your pages deserve a higher spot. Each of those steps takes time, and none of them happens the moment you hit publish.

Size and build matter too. Search Engine Land notes that large sites and JavaScript-heavy sites can take nine times longer for Google to crawl, process and reassess changes.

Rankings can also move backwards before they move forwards. A Google patent on rank transition, as cited in Search Engine Land's guide, describes a page's rank decreasing for about 20 days, then settling into its new steady-state value roughly 70 days after a positive link-based change.

[Google's core updates documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates) says some changes can take effect in a few days. It also says it could take several months for Google's systems to learn and confirm that a site as a whole is producing helpful, reliable, people-first content.

Knowing this means a quiet first few weeks is no reason to pull the pin on your SEO.

## How long does a brand-new website take to rank?

A brand-new website usually takes the longest to rank, because Google has no history to judge it on. [Ahrefs reports](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-seo-take) that Google's John Mueller said it could take up to a year for Google to figure out where to rank new sites.

Mueller made a similar point in comments reported in [Search Engine Journal's coverage of new site rankings](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-rankings-for-new-sites-could-fluctuate-for-up-to-a-year/372109). He said that for a website a couple of months to a year old, things settle down into a stable state over the course of a year.

An established site starts ahead, because Google already knows it exists and visits it regularly. Improvements to a site with a track record have a foundation to build on, while a new site is still earning that trust.

Picture a tiler in Robina who launches a new website this month. Expect the rankings to wobble for much of the first year, and judge progress on the overall trend rather than any single week.

If you're launching a new site, plan your budget around a year of steady work and you won't be caught out by the early ups and downs.

## Which SEO timeline figures are fact and which are opinion?

Most SEO timeline figures online blend Google statements, industry guides and polls without saying which is which. The table below labels each source by the kind of evidence it actually is.

Google's Maile Ohye has stated that SEO needs four months to a year to first implement improvements and then see potential benefit, as cited in Search Engine Land's guide. That lines up closely with Mueller's comments on new sites.

Ahrefs' figure comes from polls of SEO practitioners on LinkedIn and X, where respondents said SEO typically takes three to six months to show results. That tells you what people in the industry believe, which is useful context, although a poll is not a controlled study.

None of these sources is a controlled experiment, so treat every number as a planning range rather than a promise.

The sources broadly agree on months, not weeks, so plan for a range and judge the trend.

The useful move is to separate Google noticing your changes from enough enquiries to notice in your bank account. The first comes well before the second, and the table below shows what to check while you wait.

Here is what to track if you're busy on the tools and only have a few minutes a week.

- Ask every new customer how they found you and write the answer on the job sheet, so you can see search enquiries grow with your own eyes.
- Watch impressions in Google Search Console, because they rise before clicks and calls do.
- Check which suburbs and services you appear for, so you know the right jobs are finding you.

## How long does local SEO take compared with national SEO?

Local and national SEO follow the same broad three to 12 month ranges, because none of the sources above gives a separate timeline for local search. The real difference is the size of the field you're competing in.

A plumber in Nerang chasing local jobs mostly competes with other plumbers who service Nerang, while a national business competes with everyone in the country selling the same thing.

Your Google Business Profile does a lot of the lifting in local search. Our guide to [optimising your Google Business Profile](/insights/google-business-profile-optimisation) walks through the settings that matter for Gold Coast tradies.

We also treat a complete profile as the main route for your local business details to reach AI answers, so the same work serves both Google Maps and AI search.

Reviews feed the same engine. If you find it awkward to ask, our scripts for [asking customers for Google reviews](/insights/how-to-ask-customers-for-google-reviews) cover the timing and the rules the ACCC enforces.

Start with your local area and you give your effort a smaller, more winnable field while the bigger search terms build.

## Does your trade and suburb change the timeline?

Your trade and suburb change the timeline, because competition decides how long it takes to climb. Search Engine Land's six to 12 month range applies to competitive industries.

If your trade has many businesses chasing the same searches in your area, plan for that longer end of the range.

An electrician in Southport chasing 'electrician Gold Coast' is up against every electrician on the coast. The same electrician targeting 'switchboard upgrade Southport' faces a much smaller field.

The smart play is to build specific service-and-suburb pages first, then work up to the broad terms.

Those specific pages can bring in real jobs while the competitive terms take their time.

## How does SEO compare with Google Ads?

Google Ads can put you at the top of the page as soon as your campaign is approved and running, while SEO takes months to build. The trade-off is that ads stop the moment you stop paying, and SEO keeps working.

Many trades run both, using ads for the phone this week and SEO for the phone next year. Ads also help you test which services sell before you invest in pages for them.

Used together, the two let you fill quiet weeks now while building enquiries you don't pay for every click on.

| When you stop paying | Your pages stay in Google's index and keep working | Your ads stop showing |
| How you pay | You invest in pages and improvements, with no charge per click | You pay for each click |
| Best use | Long-term enquiries and a compounding asset | Immediate leads and testing which services sell |

## Does showing up in ChatGPT or AI Overviews happen faster?

In our experience, showing up in ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews doesn't happen faster than ranking on Google. AI answers tend to draw on pages that are already indexed and ranking, so there is no separate shortcut.

Our position is that generative engine optimisation is SEO with better-structured answers, rather than a separate product. That's why we don't sell AI visibility as an add-on.

No one has published a separate timeline for AI citations that I'd stand behind, so I won't invent one for you. What I can tell you is the order of work we follow to give a page its best chance of being cited.

- Rank the page first, because in our experience AI answers pull from the same pool of pages that ordinary search does.
- Answer the customer's question in visible text high on the page, because AI systems lift answers from text they can read, not from images.
- Give each question worth owning its own page, so there's a clear page to cite for cost, process, timing and comparisons.
- Leave AI search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot unblocked, because blocking a search crawler removes your site from that engine's answers.
- Skip paid extras sold as AI shortcuts, such as hand-built llms.txt files or schema pitched as a citation booster, because we don't have evidence they earn citations.

## How do I know if my SEO isn't working?

You should worry when nothing moves at all after several months, not when rankings wobble in the first few weeks. Google's core updates documentation allows several months for its systems to confirm a site's quality, so early swings are normal.

The same documentation adds that if you see no effect after a few months of improvements, you may need to wait for the next core update before the changes show.

These are the signs that something needs a closer look.

- Your new pages still aren't indexed after several weeks, which usually points to a technical problem rather than a need for patience.
- Impressions in Search Console stay flat after three to six months, which is the window where early signals normally show.
- Your provider can't show you what they changed each month, which makes progress impossible to judge.
- You rank for terms nobody in your service area actually searches, which brings traffic without jobs.
- Your website itself is slow, dated or hard to use, and our list of [signs your website needs a redesign](/insights/when-to-redesign-your-website) will help you tell.

## What can legitimately speed SEO up, and what puts you at risk?

You can't force Google's timeline, but you can stop wasting it. The fastest legitimate gains come from fixing what's holding the site back and giving Google clear pages to rank.

Our [complete SEO checklist for 2026](/insights/complete-seo-checklist-2026) covers every step in order, and these are the ones that matter most early on.

- Fix technical problems first, so Google can crawl and index every page you build.
- Build one page per service and suburb you want work in, so each search has a page that answers it.
- Complete your Google Business Profile and keep earning genuine reviews, because local searches lean heavily on both.
- List your business consistently on Australian directories such as ProductReview, Localsearch, Oneflare, Yellow Pages and Trustpilot, so your details match everywhere customers look.
- Earn real local mentions in local press, genuine best-of lists and industry sites, because third-party recognition is hard for competitors to copy.
- Avoid bought links, fake reviews, invented brand mentions and pages churned out in bulk, because shortcuts like these can undo months of honest work.
