## What is a fractional CTO, in plain terms?

A fractional CTO does the same job as a full-time chief technology officer, only for part of the week. [This Melbourne CTO services firm](https://ok200.net/cto-services) describes that job as setting technical direction, making architecture and vendor decisions, overseeing developers and translating technical work for boards and investors.

The word fractional describes the hours, not the experience. [A guide to what a fractional CTO is](https://www.aitalentondemand.com.au/article/what-is-a-fractional-cto) notes these are experienced executives, often with 15 to 25 years in technology leadership, who work across two or three organisations at the same time.

The title often gets mixed up with others. A CIO traditionally looks after internal systems and IT operations, a vCIO is that role delivered virtually, often by an IT support company, and a web developer builds what someone else has decided.

The CTO is the person who decides what gets built, bought, connected or switched off.

For you, that means one experienced person owns the technology decisions, so you are no longer making them alone between invoices.

## How is a fractional CTO different from IT support, a web developer or a marketing agency?

A fractional CTO owns the decisions, while IT support, web developers and marketing agencies each own one part of the delivery. The table below shows where each role starts and stops for a typical service business.

Most trade businesses already pay for some of these roles without anyone sitting above them to make sure the pieces fit together.

Knowing who owns what stops you paying two suppliers for the same job, or none for the job that matters.

*Who does what in a small business technology setup*

| Who you hire | What they own | What they usually leave to someone else |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fractional CTO | Which systems you use, how they connect, which suppliers you pay and what gets built next | Day-to-day support tickets and hands-on marketing campaigns |
| IT consultant | Specific advice or setup work on a defined technical problem | Owning your overall technology direction once the job is done |
| Interim CTO | The full CTO role, full-time, for a set period such as covering a gap while a permanent hire is found | Staying on once the gap is filled |
| IT support or managed service provider | Keeping computers, email, devices and security running | Deciding whether your booking or quoting software is the right choice |
| Web developer | Building and maintaining the website to a brief | Setting the brief and connecting the site to the rest of the business |
| Digital marketing or SEO agency | Rankings, Google Business Profile, content and ads that bring in enquiries | Choosing back-office software such as CRM, quoting and invoicing tools |
| Full-time CTO | Everything a fractional CTO does, plus leading an in-house technology team every day | Very little, but the cost only makes sense once software is central to the business |

## What does a fractional CTO actually do for a trade business?

For a business with no developers, the work is less about architecture diagrams and more about the everyday decisions that quietly cost you jobs and hours.

Here is what that looks like week to week for an electrician, plumber, builder or allied health clinic.

- They compare booking and job management systems against how your team actually works, so you are not stuck with software that fights your process for years.
- They connect your quoting, invoicing and job tools so a job entered once flows through to the invoice, instead of being typed into several places.
- They sort out email, calendars and file sharing, and if you are weighing that up now, our [Google Workspace comparison for Australian small businesses](/insights/google-workspace-vs-microsoft-365) walks through the choice.
- They check whether your website turns visitors into enquiries and decide whether it needs fixing or replacing, using signs like those in our guide to the [red flags that say it is time to redesign your website](/insights/when-to-redesign-your-website).
- They set up lead tracking so you can see which calls and forms came from Google, ads or referrals, and stop paying for channels that do not deliver.
- They audit software subscriptions and supplier contracts, cancelling what overlaps and renegotiating what stays.

## Does a trade or small service business actually need a fractional CTO?

Most trade and local service businesses do not need a full fractional CTO retainer, but almost all of them face the decisions a CTO would make.

For context, an [Australian Bureau of Statistics survey of Australian businesses](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/business-adoption-artificial-intelligence-accelerates-2024-25) found around 12 per cent reported using AI in their workplace in 2024–25. Adoption was higher among businesses with more employees, while uptake among small and micro businesses was lower, at around 11 per cent.

That figure measures AI use, not access to advice, but it is a reminder that small businesses tend to take up new technology more slowly than larger ones.

Run through this self-check before you spend anything.

- Your team enters the same job details into more than one system.
- You chose your booking or job software years ago and nobody has checked whether it still fits.
- Your website gets visits, but the phone does not ring.
- You cannot say which marketing channel produced last month's jobs.
- Your software subscriptions have crept up and you are not sure what each one does.
- You are about to sign up for new software or a custom build, and nobody independent has reviewed it.
- If several of these sound familiar, it is worth getting an independent view before your next technology decision, and if none apply, you can keep your money in the business for now.

## What size of business should consider one?

The right time to consider fractional CTO help depends less on turnover and more on how much your business relies on its systems.

Competitor guides answer this in startup terms, such as funding rounds and developer headcount. For a trade or service business, these signals are more useful.

- Several staff now use the same systems every day, so a poor choice slows down the whole team rather than just you.
- You run a number of separate tools for bookings, quoting, invoicing, rostering and marketing that do not talk to each other.
- Software has become central to how you deliver the service, such as online booking, customer portals or job tracking in the field.
- You are planning a custom build, a major platform change or a new website, and the decision will be expensive to undo.
- If only one or two of these apply, a single scoped piece of advice is likely enough, so you only pay for the help your stage of business needs.

## How does a fractional CTO engagement work, and can you cancel?

Fractional CTO help is usually bought in one of three ways: by the day, on a monthly retainer, or as a fixed project. The table below shows how each model works and when it fits.

Because a fractional CTO often works across two or three organisations at once, you are buying a share of their time. Agree up front how much of it you get and how quickly they respond.

For a trade business, I suggest starting with a scoped project, such as choosing job management software or auditing your subscriptions, and only moving to ongoing advice once that first piece has proved its worth.

Starting small lets you judge the advice on a real decision before you commit to ongoing fees.

## When do you move from a fractional CTO to a full-time CTO?

You move to a full-time CTO when technology becomes the product, or when the people building it need daily leadership.

The usual signs are that you employ in-house developers, you are building your own software platform, or investors and a board expect regular technology reporting.

Most trade and service businesses never reach that point, and staying with part-time advice is a perfectly sensible place to be.

That way you get senior judgement on the decisions that matter without carrying an executive salary you do not need.

## How do you vet a fractional CTO?

Vet a fractional CTO on track record with businesses like yours and on how clearly they explain things, because you will rely on their judgement for decisions you cannot check yourself.

These questions and red flags will help you sort the useful advisers from the expensive ones.

- Ask them to explain your options in plain English, because if they cannot explain it to you, they will not be able to brief your team either.
- Ask for references from businesses your size, since experience with a funded software startup does not automatically transfer to a builder or a physio clinic.
- Ask whether they earn commissions from any software they recommend, so you know the advice is independent.
- Ask what happens if you part ways, including who holds your accounts, passwords and data.
- Treat it as a red flag if the first recommendation is a custom build when an off-the-shelf tool would do the job.
- Treat it as a red flag if the contract locks you in before they have delivered anything.
- Asking these questions up front protects your money and keeps you in control of your own systems.
