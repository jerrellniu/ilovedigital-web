## What should a small business post on social media?

A small business should post its real work, its real people and real answers to the questions customers ask. That's content no competitor can copy, and you create it every day you're on the tools.

Facebook is still the place to start for most local businesses. A Meta survey reported in the [ACCC's Digital Platform Services Inquiry report on social media services](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/Digital%20platforms%20services%20inquiry%20-%20Interim%20report%206%20-%20Report%20on%20social%20media%20services_0.pdf) found approximately 83% of new Australian small and medium enterprises used Facebook to help start their business.

The same ACCC report puts Facebook at more than 21 million monthly active users in Australia, more than 80% of the total population. Instagram has more than 10 million.

Your next customer is almost certainly on one of those platforms. The posts that win them over are the ones that show you turn up, do tidy work and know what you're talking about.

## How do you turn one job into five posts?

You turn one job into five posts by taking photos and a short clip at each stage of the job, then posting each stage separately across the week.

Picture a Gold Coast electrician doing a switchboard upgrade on an older home in Palm Beach. That single morning holds a full week of content if the phone comes out at the right moments.

Space the posts out across the week rather than posting all five on the same afternoon. By the time the fifth one goes up, you'll have finished more jobs to draw from.

The same method works for a plumber in Burleigh Heads, a landscaper in Nerang or a cleaner in Southport. Every job has a before, a during, an after, a question and a happy customer.

With this habit, you'll never sit at the kitchen table at night wondering what to post.

## Should you post the same thing on Google Business Profile, Facebook and Instagram?

You can use the same job photo on all three, but each platform needs a different caption and purpose because each one reaches a different person.

Google Business Profile reaches people who are already searching for your service. According to [Google's help guide to Business Profile updates](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169?hl=en-GB), an update can include a description, a photo, a video and an action button with a link, and you can schedule updates to publish later or on a recurring weekly, monthly or custom schedule.

Google also archives posts older than six months that don't have a set date range. A profile with no recent updates looks quiet next to the competitor listed beside you.

Facebook reaches locals who already know you, your past customers and their neighbours. Instagram suits visual trades, where the finished work does the selling.

## What do you post when there are no new jobs to show?

When work is quiet, post from what you already know and what you've already done. Quiet weeks are the best time to answer questions and show the people behind the business.

- Post an old job from your camera roll, with a line about why you did it the way you did.
- Answer a question a customer asked you on the phone this month in a few plain sentences.
- Share a seasonal reminder, such as checking gutters and roof fixings before Gold Coast storm season.
- Introduce a team member, or show the van and tools being cleaned and serviced.
- Explain what a typical job involves, from the first call to the clean-up, so a new customer knows what to expect.
- Show a common problem you get called in to fix, without naming or shaming the homeowner.

## What can a solo tradie post with just a phone?

A solo tradie can post everything in this guide with a phone and the habit of taking photos at the start, middle and end of each job. You don't need editing software, a budget or any filming skill.

Real photos from your own jobs will convince a local customer far more than any stock image or designed graphic.

- Wipe the camera lens before every photo, because a smudged lens makes job photos look hazy.
- Shoot in daylight where you can, and stand back far enough to show the whole job.
- Take the before photo from the same spot as the after photo so the change is obvious.
- Record a short clip with the phone held steady, talking the way you would to the customer.
- Write captions the way you speak, naming the job and the suburb.

## Do tradies need TikTok or Instagram Reels?

Most local tradies don't need TikTok to get booked, and Facebook plus a well-kept Google Business Profile is a strong place to start. Add Instagram Reels if you're comfortable on camera and your work looks good on video.

Reels suit trades with a visible transformation, such as landscaping, painting, tiling, pressure cleaning and renovations. A short clip of a stained driveway coming up clean shows the result faster than any caption can.

If filming feels like a chore, keep your effort where your customers already are. The ACCC figures above show Facebook and Instagram already reach most Australians.

Putting your time into the platforms that matter means you get the reach without burning your evenings on video.

## How often should a small business post?

A small business should post as often as it can keep up all year, because a steady rhythm beats a burst of posts followed by months of silence. For most busy tradies, that means a weekly Google Business Profile update and a few social posts a week.

No verified figure proves that a set number of posts wins more work, so be wary of anyone who quotes one. What you can rely on is Google's own rule that undated posts are archived after six months.

Google's recurring schedule makes the rhythm easy. Write a month of updates on a rainy day and set them to publish weekly.

A rhythm you can keep is worth more than a perfect plan you drop by March.

## What's the difference between posts that get likes and posts that get calls?

Posts that get calls name the service, the suburb and the next step, while posts that get likes usually show the person behind the business. Both have a place in your feed, and the booking posts are the ones that fill the diary.

- A booking post says what you did, where you did it and how to book, such as a finished deck in Robina with a button to call.
- An engagement post shows personality, such as the team's smoko spot or the apprentice's first solo job.
- Every Google Business Profile update should carry an action button, because the people seeing it are already looking to book.

## Is it safe to post customer photos, reviews and before and after shots?

It's safe to post customer photos and reviews when you ask first, keep private details out of frame and quote customers exactly as they wrote it. Asking for consent up front means you'll never have to take a post down.

We hold ourselves to the same rule. In August 2026 we checked a client testimonial on our own home page against what the client had actually written, found it didn't match, and corrected it to the verbatim text.

Our guide to [asking customers for Google reviews and the rules the ACCC enforces](/insights/how-to-ask-customers-for-google-reviews) covers reviews in more detail.

Getting consent right protects your reputation and keeps your customers happy to recommend you.

- Ask the homeowner before you photograph their home, and tell them where the photo will appear.
- Keep house numbers, number plates, family photos and anything else identifying out of the shot.
- Never post children, including your own kids in a family business, without a parent's clear permission.
- Quote reviews word for word, and share them only where the customer is happy for you to.
- Take before and after photos in similar light and from the same angle so the result isn't exaggerated.

## How do you know if your posts are working?

Your posts are working when new customers mention them, message you through them or call you from your Google profile. Likes alone don't tell you that.

Ask every new caller how they found you, and keep a simple tally in your phone notes or job app. After a few months you'll know which platform earns its keep.

That's the measure I use with clients. I've managed organic social media for Phil Strasser at Butchers Pride since December 2025, and in his Google review he wrote that the work "has produced organic leads and engagement with clear long-term goals specific to my business."

When you track where your calls come from, you can stop posting where it doesn't pay and put your time where it does.
