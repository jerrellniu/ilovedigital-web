## Does a trade business actually need to take payments online?

Most trade businesses don't need to take payments online, because the job is priced after someone has seen the switchboard, the roof or the leaking pipe. What your website has to do is turn a Google search into a phone call, a quote request or a booking.

Your customers are certainly online. The Australia Post report found 82% of Australian households shopped online in 2025, which is 9.8 million households, and online retail spend grew from $72.5b in 2024 to $82.6b in 2025.

That data measures retail spending, so read it as proof that customers expect to find a business and act quickly online. For a tradie, acting quickly means a clear call button and a short quote form.

The same report found 81% of shoppers shop around for deals. That's a good reason for a trade site to lead with reviews, real photos and response times, because those are the things a cheaper competitor can't copy.

Get the enquiry path right first, and you'll win more of the jobs people are already searching for.

## Is there a middle option between a simple website and ecommerce?

Yes, a booking site that takes a deposit or call-out fee online sits between the two. The customer picks a time, pays a set amount and gets a confirmation, and you never deal with a cart, a catalogue or a shipping label.

This suits jobs with a fixed starting price, such as an aircon service, a pest inspection, a gas appliance check or a regular pool clean. It also helps with no-shows, because a customer who has already paid has a good reason to be home when the van pulls up.

Many booking tools and payment providers can add this to a simple site, which keeps the build much closer to a lead-gen site than to a store.

A deposit setup still counts as selling online, so the consumer rules covered further down apply to it too.

You get paid upfront for the jobs that suit it, without taking on the running costs of a full store.

## When does ecommerce make sense for a trade business?

Ecommerce makes sense when you have something to sell that doesn't need you on site. Four cases come up regularly for trades.

Be honest about competition before you sell products. The Australia Post report found marketplace apps (69%) are now almost as common as retailer websites (78%), so a trade shop selling everyday parts competes with large marketplaces on price and delivery.

Trigahex shows what a store can do when the product is the business. Trigahex is a colour-matching card game made in Queensland, and we moved the store from Wix to Shopify and rebuilt the homepage and product page to sell.

In her Google review, written during that build engagement, Erica Wheeler of Trigahex said: "I started with a sales goal to reach within 6 months and we smashed those numbers in just a few weeks."

That result came from a business whose income is product sales. A plumber's income is labour, so the same store would mostly sit idle.

Add ecommerce only where a product or plan can sell without you, and every dollar of the build has a job to do.

- Gift vouchers suit trades with a consumer side, such as detailing, landscaping or pool care, because a customer can buy one at any hour as a present.
- Maintenance plans suit aircon, solar, pool and pest businesses, because a recurring service plan pays you ahead of the work and fills your calendar in advance.
- Spare parts and consumables suit trades with stock that customers reorder, such as filters or pool chemicals, as long as your margin survives the shipping cost.
- Branded merchandise rarely justifies a full store on its own, so a few items usually sit better on a simple site with a payment link.

## Shopify or WooCommerce: which suits a trade business selling a few items?

Shopify usually suits a trade business selling a handful of items, because it's a hosted platform where security, updates and checkout are handled for you. You pay a monthly plan and payment fees, and you spend your time on jobs instead of plugin updates.

WooCommerce suits a trade business already on WordPress that wants the shop inside its existing site. It powers over 28% of all online stores according to Creative Ground, but you or your developer look after the hosting, plugins and security.

Whichever platform you choose, confirm before the build that it handles Australian GST settings and the payment options your customers expect, such as buy now, pay later.

If you only want to sell a few vouchers or a service plan, check whether your booking or payment tool can do it on your simple site first.

Choosing a platform that matches the size of your shop keeps the running costs in line with what the shop actually earns.

## Will an online store help your Google ranking?

An online store won't lift your local rankings for trade searches on its own. Google ranks an electrician for a search like "electrician Gold Coast" on service pages, location pages, reviews and a strong Google Business Profile, and product pages for merchandise add little to that.

## What changes legally when a trade business starts selling online?

Selling online puts you under the same consumer responsibilities as a shopfront. The ACCC states that online businesses have the same responsibilities to consumers as physical businesses, including consumer guarantees and rules against false or misleading claims in advertising, on your website and on social media.

Work through these four checks before your deposits, vouchers or shop go live.

- Write a refund and cancellation policy that works alongside the consumer guarantees, and make sure it covers deposits and booking fees as well as products.
- Make sure the prices, product descriptions, photos and delivery times on your site match what you actually supply, because the rules against misleading claims apply to your website.
- Ask your accountant whether you need to be registered for GST and how GST should show on your online prices before the first sale.
- Set clear written terms for gift vouchers, service plans and recurring payments, and check them against current consumer law before you sell.

## Can you add ecommerce to a simple website later?

Yes, you can usually add ecommerce to a simple website later without a full rebuild, as long as the site sits on a platform that supports selling. Most mainstream website platforms let you add a shop or payment feature to an existing site.

A rebuild becomes more likely if your current site runs on a platform with weak commerce features, or if the design can't take a cart and product pages without breaking.

Our guide to [the red flags that mean it's time to redesign your website](/insights/when-to-redesign-your-website) helps you tell the difference.

The practical path is to start with a fast lead-gen site on a platform that can grow, then add deposits or a shop once the demand is proven.

Starting simple on the right platform means you pay for ecommerce only when your business is ready to use it.

## How do you decide which website your trade business needs?

You decide by looking at how each job gets paid. Run through these four questions and the right website type usually becomes obvious.

- Choose a simple lead-gen site if every job needs a quote or a site visit before you can price it.
- Choose a booking site with deposits if you run fixed-price call-outs or inspections and no-shows cost you money.
- Choose a full ecommerce store if you have parts, plans, vouchers or products that customers will buy without speaking to you.
- Choose a simple site on a platform that can grow if you're unsure, and add payments once you've seen real demand.
