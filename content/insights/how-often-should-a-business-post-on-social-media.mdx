## Do you really have to post every day?

Most business owners don't post every day, and you can stop feeling behind if you don't either. An [Adobe Express survey of 433 business owners](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/how-often-should-you-post-content) in June 2025 found 44% post weekly and only 18% post daily.

The pressure is real, though. The same survey found 63% of owners feel pressure to post daily and 44% feel pressure to increase their posting volume, but only 24% say their audience expects daily content.

Owners in that survey put the ideal at seven times per week, which is a lot to ask of someone who spends the day on site. Full-time marketers don't hit it either, with [HubSpot's survey of marketers](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/how-frequently-should-i-publish-on-social-media) finding 64% post less than daily and multiple times a week the most common cadence at 30.9%.

If daily posting feels out of reach, you're in the majority, and you can plan a schedule around real life instead of guilt.

## What are Australians actually doing on social media?

Australians use social media almost universally, but mostly as viewers. [ACMA's February 2026 report on how Australians communicate](https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/How%20we%20communicate%20-%20Executive%20summary%20and%20key%20findings.pdf) found 98% of adults used at least one communication or social media platform in the previous 6 months, across an average of 5.5 platforms.

Facebook remained the most widely used platform in 2025 at 78%, followed by YouTube and Facebook Messenger at 71% each, with the report recording declines for Australians aged over 25. Instagram grew from 50% to 53%, TikTok from 27% to 30% and Reddit from 22% to 25%.

The figure that matters most for a trade business is that 82% of Australians engaged with content while only 55% posted or created it. Many of the people who see your page are viewing rather than posting.

Instagram is also becoming a daily habit, with 41% of Australians using it multiple times a day in 2025, up from 37% in 2024.

In our view, a recent, real job on your page gives those viewers something genuine to judge you by before they decide to call.

## How often should you post on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn?

Each platform has its own recommended range. [Buffer's 2026 posting frequency guide](https://buffer.com/resources/social-media-frequency-guide) recommends Facebook 1-2 posts a day, Instagram 3-5 a week, TikTok 2-5 a week and LinkedIn 2-5 a week.

Buffer's Facebook figure rests on a HubSpot study of over 13,500 Facebook users, as cited in Buffer's guide, which found 1-2 posts a day was the ideal frequency. On Instagram, head of product Adam Mosseri suggested in 2021 sharing a couple of in-feed posts per week and a couple of stories per day, as reported by HubSpot.

Mosseri's 2024 update, also as reported by HubSpot, says posting more often helps reach, but he warns against burnout.

Buffer's 1-2 posts a day for Facebook is the range for maximising reach, and we start solo trades at one good post a week instead, because a schedule you can keep beats one you drop, which is Buffer's own finding on consistency. Once the weekly habit holds, you can build up from there.

Facebook is still the most widely used platform in Australia, at 78% of adults in ACMA's figures, which makes it a sensible starting point for most local trades.

Focusing on one platform you can keep up with puts your work in front of the people most likely to book you.

## What's the difference between an ideal and a sustainable posting schedule?

An ideal schedule is the one research says gets the most reach, and a sustainable schedule is the one you'll still be keeping when work gets busy. For a trade business, we plan around the sustainable one.

Buffer's guide reports that a consistent posting schedule, even with fewer posts per week, performs better than posting daily and disappearing.

For busy tradies, we set a weekly post on your main platform as the floor, alongside a weekly Google Business Profile update. Once that habit sticks, we build towards the bottom of Buffer's ranges, such as three Instagram posts a week.

- Start with one platform. Choose the platform your customers use most and post there every week before you add a second.
- Keep your Google Business Profile updated weekly. Add a recent job, photo or offer so anyone searching sees a business that's working.
- Add more posts only when it's easy. Increase your schedule once you have a backlog of job photos and the weekly habit is sticking.
- A rhythm you can keep quietly builds trust with every customer who checks you out.

Fewer, better posts are the approach we recommend for a trade business. Photos and videos of real jobs show people your actual work, which generic quotes and stock photos can't do.

Buffer's finding backs the steady approach, because a consistent schedule with fewer posts performs better than posting daily and disappearing. Mosseri, as reported by HubSpot, also warns against burnout.

## What counts as a post?

Anything a customer can see that shows recent activity helps you look active, though each platform treats formats differently.

- Feed posts count as your core schedule. These are your main posts on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn, and they're what Buffer's frequency ranges refer to.
- Stories count on Instagram and Facebook. Mosseri's 2021 advice, as reported by HubSpot, paired a couple of in-feed posts per week with a couple of stories per day, so Stories are a lighter way to show a day on site.
- Google Business Profile updates count as their own channel. They appear on your Google listing rather than in a social feed, so they need their own weekly slot.
- Review replies help you look active on Google. A reply under each review shows searchers that a real person runs the business and cares about the result.
- Mixing lighter formats with your feed posts keeps you visible without adding a heavy workload.

## Do B2B trades need a different posting schedule from consumer-facing businesses?

The platform choice matters more than the frequency. If most of your work comes from builders or other businesses, LinkedIn is worth testing, and Buffer recommends 2-5 posts a week there.

If you mostly work for homeowners, or you run a retail or hospitality business, Facebook and Instagram are the natural place to start, given Facebook reaches 78% of Australian adults and Instagram use grew to 53% in 2025.

Matching the platform to your buyer means every post lands in front of someone who might actually hire you.

## How does posting frequency work with paid social ads?

Paid ads and organic posts do different jobs, so we don't treat running ads as a reason to stop posting. An ad puts you in front of new people, and anyone who taps through to your page will see whatever is on it.

In our view, a current feed of real jobs gives every ad click somewhere credible to land.

A steady weekly rhythm keeps your page ready for every person your ad budget brings in.

## Should a brand-new account post more often than an established one?

With a brand-new account, our practice is to add a handful of recent jobs, your service area and a few reviews or testimonials before you start promoting the page, then settle into your sustainable rhythm. That's how we work, not a research finding.

An established account with a history of work can hold a steady weekly pace. In both cases, consistency is the priority, which is Buffer's point about fewer posts performing better than posting daily and disappearing.

Building that base once means your page does its job every time someone checks you out.
