## What does Google Business Profile optimisation actually involve?

Optimising a Google Business Profile means making every part of your listing accurate, complete and current. That covers your business name, phone number, website, service areas, categories, services, hours, photos and reviews.

Your name, address and phone details, often called NAP, need to match everywhere your business appears, from your website footer to Localsearch and Yellow Pages.

Your profile is one part of a wider search setup, and [our 2026 SEO checklist for AI search](/insights/complete-seo-checklist-2026) covers the website side in the same plain terms. Get these basics right and you give Google fewer reasons to doubt you and more reasons to show you.

- Verify the profile. Verify it under the business owner's own Google account so you never lose access when a staff member or an old agency moves on.
- Pick your categories. Choose the primary category that matches the work that earns you the most money, then add secondary categories for the other work you genuinely do.
- List your services. Add each service you offer with a short, plain description, such as switchboard upgrades or hot water system replacement.
- Set your service areas. List the suburbs and regions you actually travel to, such as Robina, Southport and Coomera.
- Add real photos. Take real photos of the van, the team and the finished job. Your real photos are far more impactful, authentic and likely to convince a visitor than stock photos. ## How do I verify a Google Business Profile with no shopfront?

Google verifies profiles by postcard, phone, email or instant verification, and [this Google Business Profile guide for tradies](https://fergus.com/tradehub/blog/google-business-profile-tradies/) notes that a postcard typically arrives within five days.

Google decides which options it offers you, so use whichever one appears on your screen. Once verified, keep the profile active with a steady cadence rather than daily updates, as explained in our guide on [how often a business should post on social media](/insights/how-often-should-a-business-post-on-social-media).

If you run the business from home, a van or a ute, set the profile up as a business that goes to customers rather than one customers visit. Tradies without a public-facing office should select 'I deliver goods and services to my customers' and list their service areas instead of a shopfront address.

If the postcard route comes up, use an address where you reliably receive mail, and hold off on other edits while you wait.

Verify once, properly, and you won't be fighting to win back control of your own listing later.

## Should I use a service-area listing or a storefront, and does hiding my home address hurt?

A storefront listing shows an address customers can visit, while a service-area listing hides your address and shows the region you cover instead.

For a plumber working from a home in Nerang and covering the Gold Coast, a service-area listing is the honest setup, and it matches the option recommended for tradies above.

Listing a home address as a shopfront when nobody can walk in creates a mismatch between your profile and how you really work, and mismatches invite problems.

Hiding your address keeps your family home private and keeps your profile consistent with the way your business actually runs.

*Storefront listing compared with a service-area listing*

| Question | Storefront Listing | Service-Area Listing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who does it suit? | Showrooms, workshops and offices that customers visit | Trades who travel to the job from a home, depot or van |
| Is the address shown? | Yes, on the map and on the profile | No, it stays hidden from the public |
| What do you list? | Your street address and opening hours | The suburbs and regions you cover, plus your hours |
| What's a Gold Coast example? | A tile showroom in Burleigh Heads | An electrician based in Coomera covering the northern Gold Coast |

## Which categories should my trade pick, and can I have more than one?

Pick one primary category that describes your main paid work, then add secondary categories for the other services you genuinely offer.

An electrician would choose Electrician as the primary category and a plumber would choose Plumber. A builder should choose the category closest to their main job, such as Home builder or General contractor.

Add secondary categories only for work you actually take on, such as solar installation or air conditioning for an electrician.

Choose your primary category carefully and then leave it alone, because a primary category change is [one of the profile edits that can trigger a suspension](https://searchscope.com.au/google-business-profile/optimisation/).

The right categories put you in front of people searching for the exact job you want more of.

## What has Google retired that older guides still tell you to do?

Google shut down Business Profile chat globally on 31 July 2024, along with call history, so there is no messaging feature left to switch on.

Google has also been winding down the customer Q&A feature. Its AI increasingly answers profile questions automatically by pulling from your listing, reviews, website and other public data.

Some Australian guides still point to messaging, and [one 2025 Australian optimisation guide](https://distl.com.au/insights/google-business-profile-optimisation-australia-2025/) promotes Google's WhatsApp integration for direct messaging on a profile. That contradicts the report that messaging was shut down, so don't build your enquiry process around it.

Put your effort into a clear phone number, a working contact form on your website and quick call-backs instead.

Because Google's AI now answers questions from your listing and reviews, the details you write there do the job Q&A used to do.

Skip the retired features and every minute you spend on your profile goes into something customers can still see.

## Which profile edits can get my Google Business Profile suspended?

Changes to your business name, address, primary category, website URL or phone number can trigger an account suspension.

None of those edits is off limits, but each one should be made on purpose, one at a time, and only when the change is real.

A common trap is stuffing the business name with suburbs or keywords, such as 'Smith Plumbing Gold Coast Emergency Plumber' when the registered name is Smith Plumbing.

- Keep your name exact. Use the same business name that appears on your registration, signage and invoices, with no added keywords or suburbs.
- Change one thing at a time. If you move depot and change your number, make the edits separately rather than in one sitting.
- Keep proof on file. Save photos of your signed van, your trade licence and a recent business document in case Google asks you to prove who you are.
- Use one website address. Point the profile to your final homepage or service page, never to a link that redirects somewhere else.
- Handle these edits with care and your profile stays live while your competitors' listings get flagged.

## What happens if my profile gets suspended, and how do I get it back?

A suspension can take your listing off Google Maps and search, so act on it the same day you notice.

Start by checking what changed recently, since the edits listed above are known triggers.

Put those details back to what matches your real business, then request a review through your Business Profile and upload your proof, such as van photos, your licence and a business document.

Don't create a new profile while you wait, because a second listing for the same business creates a duplicate and a bigger problem.

Work through it calmly and in order, and you give yourself the best chance of getting the listing back without starting from scratch.

## How do multi-van or multi-location trades avoid duplicate profiles?

Run one profile for each genuine base that customers are served from, and never one profile per van or per suburb.

Several vans leaving one Molendinar depot are still one business at one base, so they share one profile with a combined service area.

If you genuinely run a second base, such as a Brisbane branch with its own staff, it can carry its own profile with its own phone number and its own page on your website.

Search Maps for your business name and any old phone numbers to find duplicates, then report or merge them rather than leaving them to confuse customers.

Keep every profile under one owner account so nothing is lost when a manager or an agency leaves.

A clean set of profiles means every review and every call lands on the listing that's working for you.
