## Key Points

- Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.
- Google's guidelines say service-area businesses such as a plumber working from home should hide their address, and service areas shouldn't extend beyond about 2 hours' drive from the base.

## What does Google Business Profile optimisation involve for a trade business?

Optimising your profile means making every part of the listing accurate, complete and current, from the name and phone number to the categories, services, service areas, hours, photos and reviews.

Google's [ranking tips for local results](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) say businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show in local results, so completeness is the first job, not an extra.

Think of it as dressing a shop window. A tidy window with the lights on and the right hours invites people in, and a dusty one with the wrong details sends them next door.

Your profile is one part of a wider search setup, and [our 2026 SEO checklist for AI search](/insights/complete-seo-checklist-2026) covers the website side in the same plain terms. If you want customers who ask an AI assistant to find you as well, our guide on [how to get recommended by ChatGPT](/insights/how-to-get-recommended-by-chatgpt) explains what helps.

- Claim the profile under the business owner's own Google account so you never lose access when a staff member or agency moves on.
- Choose the primary category that matches the work you most want more of, then add others only for work you genuinely do.
- List each service with a short, plain description, such as switchboard upgrades or hot water system replacement.
- Set service areas that match the suburbs you actually travel to, such as Robina, Southport and Coomera.
- Keep reviews coming in every week and reply to each one, good or bad.

## What actually ranks a business in the map pack?

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence.

Prominence is based partly on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews it has, and Google lists replying to reviews as a tip.

Spend your time on the factors you control and you stop wasting money on the ones you can't.

*The three local ranking factors Google names, and what a tradie can control*

| Factor | What It Means in Plain Terms | What You Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevance | How well your listing matches the job someone searched for | Accurate categories, services and a clear description |
| Distance | How far your business is from the searcher or the suburb they searched | Honest service areas, since you can't move your base to rank |
| Prominence | How well known your business is, based partly on links and reviews | Earning reviews, replying to them and getting genuine links from other websites |

## Can I pay Google to rank higher on Maps?

No, Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Google does sell advertising, but an ad is a paid placement, not a better ranking for your free listing.

Anyone promising a guaranteed map pack position for a fee is selling something Google says doesn't exist.

Know the difference and you can judge every paid offer on its own merits without being misled about your free listing.

Signing up for ads doesn't change how your free listing ranks, because Google says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking.

Eligibility, screening and availability for your trade and suburb are set by Google, so check Google's own Local Services Ads pages before you apply rather than relying on an agency summary. We haven't cited those pages here, and we don't repeat cost-per-lead figures because none we've seen are sourced.

## Can I have a Google listing working from home, and should I hide my address?

Yes, you can have a listing without a shopfront, and Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say service-area businesses should hide their address, using a plumber working from home as the example.

Set the profile up as a business that goes to its customers, then list the areas you cover instead of showing your street address.

Showing a home address as a shopfront when nobody can walk in creates a mismatch between your listing and how your business really works.

Hiding your address keeps your family home private and keeps your profile inside Google's rules.

## How many suburbs can I list, and why can't I rank far from my base?

List the suburbs you genuinely work in, and keep them within the limit in Google's guidelines, which say service areas shouldn't extend beyond about 2 hours' drive from your base.

Distance is one of the three ranking factors, so even a suburb inside your service area will be harder to rank in the further it sits from where you're based.

A Nerang plumber can list Brisbane suburbs within that limit, but should expect to rank most strongly closer to home. That's Google's distance factor at work, not a fault in your listing.

Set a realistic area and your listing shows up where you can actually get to the job on time.

## How do I audit my listing like a customer walking past?

Walk past your own shopfront by searching your business name on your phone, the way a customer in Robina or Springwood would.

Judge what you see the way you'd judge a shop window, asking whether it looks open, tidy and run by someone who still answers the phone.

- Check the name matches your van and invoices exactly, because Google's guidelines say it must match the real-world name on your signage and stationery.
- Tap the phone number and confirm it rings a phone you answer, because the same guidelines require phone numbers to be under the business's direct control.
- Check the hours before every public holiday and the Christmas shutdown, because wrong hours can send a ready customer to the next listing.
- Look at the first few photos and ask whether they show your work, your van and your team, or just a logo.
- Read your newest reviews and make sure each one has a reply.
- Tap the website link and make sure it lands on a working page that matches the services on your listing.

## How do I claim and verify a listing that already exists or that an old agency controls?

If your business already shows on Maps, claim that listing rather than creating a new one, because Google's guidelines say there should be one profile per business.

If an old agency or a former staff member controls it, ask them in writing to make your own Google account the primary owner, then remove their access once the transfer is done.

If they won't respond, use the request-access option Google shows when you try to claim a listing someone else manages, and keep a copy of every request you send.

Verification is the step where Google checks you really run the business. Other Australian guides describe several methods, including video verification, but we haven't yet cited a Google page confirming the current list or when each method appears, so go by the options Google shows in your own dashboard.

If verification stalls, don't create a second profile and don't keep editing your details. Leave the name and phone exactly as they appear on your signage, then contact Google Business Profile support from inside your profile with your proof ready.

Own the listing yourself and you never have to fight to win back your own shopfront.

## What should my business name, categories and description say?

Your business name must match the real-world name on your signage and stationery, and it must not include taglines, hours, phone numbers, or location or service information.

A plumber trading as Smith Plumbing should appear as Smith Plumbing, not Smith Plumbing Gold Coast Emergency Plumber. Google's guidelines also say legal terms such as LTD are not permitted in the name unless real-world proof shows them as part of the name.

Google says to use the fewest categories needed, as specific as possible, and not to use categories as keywords. An electrician picks Electrician as the primary category and adds others only for work they actually take on.

The same guidelines say your description can't contain links or focus on promotions and prices. Use it to say what you do, where you work and why a customer can trust you, in the words customers really search, which [our guide to free keyword research for small business](/insights/keyword-research-for-small-business) shows you how to find.

Get these three fields right and Google knows exactly which jobs to show you for.

## Which photos work for trades?

Add real photos. Take real photos of the van, the team and the finished job, because they show a customer the actual business that will turn up at their door.

Before-and-after shots of the same job, a signed van on site and the team in uniform tell a customer more about your work than a logo can.

If your work is seasonal, such as air conditioning before summer or storm repairs, refresh your photos before the busy period so your window shows the work people are searching for.

Ask permission before photographing a customer's home, and leave out house numbers and number plates.

Good photos make a stranger feel they already know who will turn up at the door.

## How do I ask for reviews without breaking the ACCC rules?

Ask every customer for a review, the same way after every job, and never fake, buy or bribe one.

The ACCC's guidance on [online reviews for products and services](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/online-reviews-for-product-and-services) says it is against the law for a business to create fake or misleading reviews, or to arrange for others to do so. That includes staff, family and paid review sellers.

Offering incentives such as discounts or free gifts for positive reviews risks breaching the law. Any incentive must apply whatever the review says and must be disclosed.

The ACCC page shows it was last modified on 15 July 2026, so look at it again before you run any review campaign.

## How do I reply to a bad review or deal with fake reviews and fake competitors?

Reply to every review, including the bad ones, once, calmly and specifically, and offer to sort the problem out offline.

Don't hide, edit or pressure customers to remove negative reviews, because the ACCC says suppressing or editing negative reviews can mislead consumers.

If you spot a fake review, report it through your profile. You can also report fake or misleading reviews to the ACCC, though it doesn't resolve individual disputes.

If a competitor runs duplicate listings or a keyword-stuffed name, suggest an edit on Maps and point to the rule they break, such as one profile per business or a name that must match real signage.

If your suggested edit is ignored, escalate through Google's Business Redressal Complaint Form, which you can find in Google Business Profile Help, and attach dated screenshots of the listing and the rule it breaks.

A calm public reply shows the next customer how you handle problems, which is often worth more than the review itself.

## What gets a listing suspended, and how do I appeal?

Listings run into trouble when they break Google's guidelines, so the safest profile is one that follows them to the letter.

Australian guides also warn that edits to the name, address, categories, website or phone can prompt a review, so make those changes one at a time and only when they're real.

- Stuffing suburbs or services into the business name breaks the rule that the name must match your real-world signage.
- Showing a home address as a shopfront breaks the guidance that service-area businesses should hide their address.
- Running a second profile for the same business breaks the one-profile-per-business rule.
- Using a phone number you don't control breaks the rule that numbers must be under the business's direct control.
- Putting links or price promotions in the description breaks the description rules.
- To appeal, put the details back to what matches your real business, then lodge an appeal through the official appeal option Google shows on your suspended profile, uploading proof such as signed van photos, your licence and a recent business document.
- Don't create a new profile while you wait, because a duplicate turns one problem into two.

## How do I stop someone changing my details?

Keep the primary owner role on your own Google account, never a staff member's or an agency's.

A listing can have more than one person with access. Give the owner role only to someone you'd trust with the business itself, and give staff and agencies manager access so they can help with the listing without being able to take it from you.

Remove anyone's access the day they leave, and check your listing every week for changes you didn't make yourself, including updates Google has applied.

If a detail has been altered, change it back straight away and keep your proof on file in case you need to show the real details.

Guard the keys and check the window weekly, and your shopfront stays exactly the way you set it up.

## How do I track calls and clicks from the listing?

Matching details across the web make it easy for Google and customers to trust they've found the right business.
