## What is a keyword, and how is it different from how customers talk?

A keyword is the exact phrase someone types or speaks into Google, such as 'blocked drain Robina' or 'electrician Southport'.

Customers usually describe the problem they can see, like 'power keeps tripping', 'water coming through the ceiling after the storm' or 'aircon blowing warm air'.

An electrician might call that a safety switch fault, but the homeowner standing in a dark kitchen types what is in front of them.

A good keyword list holds both versions, so your pages match the words a stressed customer actually uses.

## Where can I see what customers already type, for free?

Your Google Business Profile already records the words people used to find you. Google's [Business Profile performance report](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en) includes a Searches metric, described as the search terms people used to find your business.

The report only appears for verified profiles, and the Searches figures update at the start of each month and can take up to 5 days to appear. Google also says you cannot directly manage the queries shown, so read the list as a record of real behaviour.

Google Search Console does the same job for your website. The [Performance report in Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en) shows which queries bring traffic to your site, with clicks, impressions, CTR and average position for each query.

Average position is the position of the topmost result from your site. You may not see your site when you search a listed query yourself, because results vary by time, place, device and search history.

Search Console also shows you which pages are close to the first page. Sort or filter the queries table by average position, pick out the queries sitting just past the first page of results, and check whether each one has a dedicated page or section answering it.

Where a near-miss query has no page or section of its own, write that section next. It is a phrase Google already links to your site, so it is a sensible place to start.

Your phone is the third free source. Write down the exact words customers use on calls, in texts and in quote forms before you translate them into trade language.

If your profile is not verified yet, our guide to [Google Business Profile optimisation for Gold Coast tradies](/insights/google-business-profile-optimisation) walks you through setting it up properly.

Starting here means your list is built on what real customers typed, rather than on a tool's guess.

## How do I work through my Business Profile searches report?

Work through the report in four passes: copy, group, map and flag. Here is how I would do it for a Gold Coast electrician, using illustrative terms that show the shape of a typical list rather than any client's data.

First, copy every term into a spreadsheet exactly as written, including your business name and misspellings. Then group terms that describe the same job, such as 'switchboard upgrade' and 'old fuse box replacement'.

Next, write the page each group should land on beside it. Finally, flag any group with no matching page, because that gap is your next page or section.

Working from this report means your page plan starts with the searches that already led customers to your listing.

## How do I fill the gaps with autocomplete and People Also Ask?

Open Google on your phone, type your service plus a suburb slowly, and note what autocomplete suggests. Try 'hot water system', then add 'Coomera', 'replacement' or 'cost' and watch the suggestions change.

Scroll to the People Also Ask box and the related searches at the bottom of the results. Each question there is something a customer wants answered, and a likely heading or FAQ on your site.

Google Trends, set to Queensland, shows whether interest in a service rises and falls through the year.

Google's [Keyword Planner help page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en) explains that the tool discovers keywords and shows estimates of monthly searches and the cost to target them. To use even the basic features you must complete Google Ads account setup by entering billing information, so only set it up if you are comfortable adding a card to an ads account.

Together these sources fill the gaps your own data leaves, without paying for a tool.

## How do emergency, near me, after-hours and cost searches differ?

Search intent is usually described as informational, navigational, commercial or transactional, and 'near me' is a local modifier that can be added to any of them.

When we plan pages for trades, we label problem searches as informational, brand searches as navigational, cost searches as commercial, and emergency and after-hours searches as transactional. The label tells you what the page has to do first.

Long-tail problem phrases such as 'safety switch keeps tripping' suit small trade sites, because they describe one specific problem that a single focused page can answer fully.

Writing 'near me' into every heading reads awkwardly to customers, so keep your Business Profile address or service area accurate instead.

For cost searches, explain in plain words what changes the price, even if you cannot give a fixed figure. Our guide to [writing great website copy](/insights/writing-great-website-copy) shows how to say it clearly.

Matching the page to the intent gives each visitor the answer they came for.

## What if a tool shows zero volume for a term that brings in jobs?

Keep the term if your own data shows it brings in work. Google says keywords with very low search volumes are not discoverable or forecastable in Keyword Planner and return an error, so a blank result often means the number is simply too small to report.

[Digital Debut's keyword research guide](https://www.digitaldebut.com.au/info-hub/how-to-do-keyword-research) suggests a phrase with 300 searches a month in Australia can be very valuable for a local business. A suburb phrase that turns up in your Business Profile searches is worth keeping whatever a tool says.

Run each low-volume term through these four questions. If you answer yes to the first three and no to the fourth, write it into a page.

Trusting your own enquiries over a tool's blank means you keep the phrases that pay.

- Has this phrase, or a customer using these words, brought in a job?
- Does it match a service that makes you good money?
- Is it in an area you actually want to work in?
- Does an existing page already answer it well? If yes, add the phrase to that page rather than building a new one.

## Which keyword numbers can't be trusted?

Treat every search volume figure as an estimate, because that is how Google itself describes Keyword Planner data.

Digital Debut also says free Keyword Planner shows ranges rather than exact numbers, but the Keyword Planner help page we checked does not state this, so treat it as a third-party report.

Your own reports are the best starting point, but treat them as a sample of real searches rather than a complete count.

Knowing which numbers are soft stops you building pages around figures that were never real.

## What do customers check before hiring a plumber or electrician on the Gold Coast?

A homeowner choosing a plumber or electrician on the Gold Coast can search the problem, look up what the job might cost, read your reviews and check your licence. Each of those steps is a chance to be found and chosen.

Problem searches like 'hot water system leaking' or 'lights flickering whole house' need a page or section that explains the problem in the customer's words and says when to call a professional.

Cost searches like 'switchboard upgrade cost Gold Coast' need a plain explanation of what changes the price, written in visible text on the service page.

Reviews sit on your Google Business Profile, so keep asking happy customers to leave one and reply to every review you receive.

Licences can be checked by customers. In Queensland, plumbers and drainers are licensed through the Queensland Building and Construction Commission (QBCC), and electrical contractors through the Electrical Safety Office, so show your licence number on your website and Business Profile where customers can see it.

Making every one of these checks easy gives a careful customer fewer reasons to ring the next business on the list.

## How do I stop two pages competing for one term?

Give each phrase one owner page, and check that no other page targets the same phrase in its title and main heading.

Search Console helps here. Filter the Performance report by a query, then open the Pages tab to see whether more than one URL is getting impressions for it.

If two pages share a term, merge them or rewrite one to target a different service or question, then link between them. Our [complete SEO checklist for 2026](/insights/complete-seo-checklist-2026) covers the rest of the on-page basics.

One clear owner page per phrase means your effort builds on itself instead of splitting in half.

## How many suburb pages are too many?

There is no safe number, and anyone quoting one is guessing. Sort your suburbs into three tiers instead, based on what you can honestly say about working there.

Tier 1 is suburbs where you have real jobs, real photos and a genuine local detail, and each one earns its own page. Tier 2 is suburbs you serve regularly but have little specific to say about, and they are listed on your main service page.

Tier 3 is every other suburb you will travel to, and those sit only in your Business Profile service area.

Before you build a suburb page, run through these questions. If any answer is no, keep that suburb in Tier 2 or Tier 3.

Fewer, genuine local pages give customers a reason to trust you and keep your site free of thin near-duplicates.

- Have you completed real jobs in this suburb that you can describe?
- Do you have your own photos from work there?
- Can you add a local detail that would not make sense on any other suburb page?
- Would the page still be useful if you deleted the suburb name from it?

## Is keyword research still relevant with ChatGPT and AI Overviews?

Keyword research is still worth doing, because customers describe their problem in their own words whether they type it into Google or ask ChatGPT.

Our understanding is that AI search features break a question into several related sub-questions and look for pages that answer each one, a process often called query fan-out. A question like 'who should I call to fix a leaking roof on the Gold Coast' can split into cost, timing, insurance and licensing questions.

Our position is that AI visibility is ordinary SEO with better-structured answers. So for every client we focus first on ranking the page, then on putting the answer in visible text high on the page.

## How do I check whether my business appears in AI answers?

Ask the tools the way a customer would. Type questions from your list into ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode on your phone, such as 'best electrician in Nerang for a switchboard upgrade', and note which businesses and sources appear.

Answers can change between sessions and locations, so ask the same questions each month and record the results in your spreadsheet rather than trusting a single check.

A simple monthly record shows you whether your pages and profile are starting to earn a mention.

## What's a quick keyword routine for a busy tradie?

Run this routine once a month, after your Business Profile Searches report refreshes, and do a fuller review each season or whenever you add a service.

Done regularly, this keeps your site speaking your customers' language without eating into your time on the tools.

- Open your Business Profile performance report and copy any new search terms into your spreadsheet.
- Open Search Console, sort queries by average position, and note any that sit just past the first page without a dedicated page or section.
- Read back through the month's calls, texts and quote requests and add the exact phrases customers used.
- Type your main services plus your key suburbs into Google on your phone and add any new autocomplete and People Also Ask phrases.
- Ask ChatGPT and Google's AI Mode a few customer questions and record which businesses are named.
- Pick the biggest gap and either add a section to an existing page or plan a new page for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a paid keyword tool for my small business?**
No, most trades can start with free sources. Your Google Business Profile Searches metric, the Search Console Performance report, your own call notes, autocomplete and People Also Ask show what customers type. Keyword Planner is free to use but requires billing information, and paid tool prices change, so check the vendor's own page on the day if you ever buy one.

**Why does Keyword Planner show no data for my suburb?**
Usually because the search volume is too small to report. Google says keywords with very low search volumes are not discoverable or forecastable in Keyword Planner and return an error. The Keyword Planner help page we checked also does not confirm suburb-level targeting, so rely on your Business Profile searches and Search Console queries instead.

**Where do I find what people searched to find my Google listing?**
Look at the Searches metric in your Google Business Profile performance report. It shows the search terms people used to find your business, but only for verified profiles. The figures update at the start of each month and can take up to 5 days to appear.

**How do I check a plumber's or electrician's licence in Queensland?**
Look the licence up with the body that issues it. Plumbers and drainers are licensed through the QBCC, and electrical contractors through the Electrical Safety Office. If you are the tradie, show your licence number on your website and Business Profile so customers can check it easily.

**How many suburb pages should a tradie have?**
Only as many as you can fill with real jobs, real photos and genuine local detail. Give those Tier 1 suburbs their own page, list regular suburbs on your main service page, and keep the rest in your Business Profile service area.

**Is keyword research still worth doing with ChatGPT and AI Overviews?**
