Your website is mobile friendly if a customer can open it on their phone, read it without zooming, tap once to call you and send a quote request without a struggle. The quickest way to find out is to run the checks below on the phone in your hand, from the ute or the job site.

This checklist sits under our guide to [Gold Coast website design and builds](/websites), and it is written for tradies and service businesses across the Gold Coast and South East QLD. Every check has a clear pass or fail, a fix, and the question to put to your web provider. If your site fails several checks, our [guide to choosing between a custom website and a template](/insights/custom-websites-gold-coast) helps you decide whether to rebuild or simply customise.

## Key Points

- Google indexes and ranks the mobile version of your site, crawled with its smartphone agent, so the phone version is the one Google judges you on.
- In 2025, 97 per cent of Australian adults accessed the internet via a mobile phone, up from 95 per cent in 2024, and daily mobile internet use rose to 92 per cent.
- Google says that if the mobile version of a page has less content than the desktop version, the page can lose traffic.
- Google recommends responsive design because it serves the same HTML on the same URL and is the easiest pattern to implement and maintain.
- A trade website passes on mobile when the phone number taps to call, the quote form is short, photos upload from the phone and the service area is listed in plain text.
- The Create & Grow local SEO checklist for tradies sets a mobile load time target of under 3 seconds as one of its 10 points.

## What does mobile friendly actually mean in 2026?

A mobile-friendly website works as well on a phone as it does on a computer, for your customers and for Google.

Google uses the mobile version of a site's content, crawled with its smartphone agent, for indexing and ranking, according to [Google's mobile-first indexing best practices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing).

That means mobile friendly is no longer just a layout that shrinks to fit. It means the same content, headings and photos on the phone as on desktop, with buttons a thumb can hit first time.

Get this right and the version of your site your customers use is also the version Google reads.

## How do I check my website on my phone right now?

Pick up your phone, turn Wi-Fi off and open your site the way a customer would, by searching your business name or your trade and suburb on Google and tapping through.

Work through these steps in order and note every fail as you go.

- Load your home page on mobile data, because that is how a customer standing in a flooded laundry will see it.
- Hold the phone upright and read the first screen without zooming, and count it as a fail if you need to pinch or scroll sideways.
- Find your phone number without scrolling, tap it and check the dialler opens with the right number.
- Open the menu and tap through each service page, checking every link goes where it says it does.
- Fill in your quote form as a customer would, attach a photo from your camera roll, submit it and check the enquiry lands in your inbox.
- Look for your service area and check the suburbs you want work in are listed on the phone, not only on desktop.
- Open the same page on a computer and compare the two, because anything on desktop that is missing on the phone is a content problem.
- Repeat the run on a second phone, ideally an older one or a different brand from yours.

## What are the pass and fail checks for a trade website?

Use this table to score your self-test. Each row is something a customer does on a phone, and a single fail can be enough to lose the job.

Score every row honestly and you have a fix list you can hand straight to your web provider.

*Mobile self-test for trade websites: pass or fail*

| Check | Pass | Fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading the page | Text reads at normal size with no sideways scrolling | You pinch, zoom or scroll sideways to read |
| Phone number | Visible at the top and opens the dialler with one tap | Hidden in the footer, or tapping does nothing |
| Buttons and links | Easy to hit with a thumb first time | You keep tapping the wrong link |
| Quote form | A few short fields that submit and show a confirmation | Long, cut off at the edge, or errors on submit |
| Photo upload | Attaches a photo from the camera or gallery | No upload option, or the upload fails |
| Service area | Suburbs listed in plain text on the phone | Missing on mobile or only shown inside an image |
| Loading on mobile data | Name, service and number appear quickly | Blank screen, spinning loader or stalled video |
| Pop-ups and banners | Close with one obvious tap | Cover the call button or cannot be closed |
| Content match | Same text, headings and photos as desktop | Sections missing on the phone |

## Which trade-specific checks matter most?

A tradie's website has two jobs on a phone, which are to get the call or to get the quote request.

Picture a Burleigh Heads homeowner at night with a burst flexi hose. They will not scroll to your footer or fill in a long form, so the number at the top and the short form are the whole website to them.

- Put your phone number in the header of every page and make it tap-to-call, because a customer with water through the ceiling will not go looking for it.
- Test click-to-call on an iPhone and an Android if you can, and check it dials your current mobile or office line rather than an old number.
- Keep the quote form short by asking for name, phone, suburb and a description of the job, and save everything else for the phone call.
- Let customers upload a photo of the cracked tile, the switchboard or the overgrown yard, because a good photo can save you a wasted site visit.
- List your service area in plain text, naming the suburbs and regions you cover, such as Southport, Robina, Coomera and the hinterland.
- Say near the top of the page if you do after-hours or emergency call-outs, with the number right beside it.
- Show your licence number where a customer can see it on the phone if your trade is licensed, because it answers the trust question before they ask it.

## How fast should a mobile site load, and how do I test on patchy reception?

Your site should show useful content quickly on mobile data, and the [Create & Grow local SEO checklist for tradies](https://academy.createandgrow.au/resources/local-seo-checklist) sets a mobile load time target of under 3 seconds as one of its 10 points.

Test it where your customers are. Load your site on mobile data in a spot with weak signal, such as a job site or a drive through the hinterland, and watch what appears first.

It passes when your name, your service and your phone number appear before the rest of the page finishes loading. It fails when you get a blank white screen, a spinning loader or a large video that never starts.

Oversized photos and auto-playing video are common causes, and both can usually be fixed without a rebuild.

Speed is one reason visitors give up early, and we cover the others in [why people leave your website in seconds](/insights/why-do-visitors-leave-my-website).

A fast first screen means the customer on one bar of signal still gets your number.

## Which free tools can I use, and what happened to Google's Mobile-Friendly Test?

Your phone is the best free tool you have, and the self-test above catches the problems customers actually run into.

For a second opinion, the two free tools to try are Lighthouse, which is built into the Chrome browser's developer tools on a computer, and Google's PageSpeed Insights, which runs a similar report from a web page.

Here is the honest status update. Google's standalone Mobile-Friendly Test is still linked from some tradie checklists, and at least one search result we saw showed it as retired, but we have not confirmed its current status for this post.

So do not rely on it. If a tool page does not run a fresh test on your site, move on to Lighthouse or PageSpeed Insights, and check those run properly too before you trust the result.

Read the results for problems you can act on, such as small text, tap targets that sit too close together and images that are too large, and treat the overall score as a guide rather than a pass mark.

A tool report plus your own phone test gives you a fix list you can trust.

## Does being mobile friendly affect my Google ranking?

Yes, in the way that matters most, because Google uses the mobile version of your site for indexing and ranking.

If your mobile site is missing content, broken or hard to use, that is the version Google is working from.

We have not found a Google source that says mobile friendliness directly changes your position in the map pack, so we will not claim it does. Your Google Business Profile has its own ranking work, which we cover in [our Google Business Profile guide for Gold Coast tradies](/insights/google-business-profile-optimisation).

The same caution applies to AI answers. We have no source showing that ChatGPT or Google's AI features judge mobile friendliness on its own, so we will not say they do.

What is documented is that Google reads your mobile version, and that alone is reason enough to get it right.

## Is my content hidden on mobile?

It might be, and Google takes it seriously, because if the mobile version has less content than desktop the page can lose traffic.

Google's guidance also says titles, meta descriptions, headings, structured data and image alt text should match across mobile and desktop.

Watch for primary content that only appears after a swipe, a click or typing, because Google says it will not load content that needs those actions.

These are the places content most often goes missing on a phone.

- Service descriptions get cut down to a single line on mobile to save space.
- Reviews, licence details or service areas are switched off on mobile by a theme setting.
- Photo galleries only load when someone swipes through them.
- Extra detail is only fetched when someone taps a read more button.

## Why does my site look fine on my phone but break on a customer's?

Your phone is one screen size, one browser and often a fast connection, and your customer's phone may be none of those.

Check each of these before you decide your site is fine.

- Screen sizes vary, so a layout that fits a large phone can overflow on a small or older one.
- Browsers behave differently, so test in Safari on an iPhone and Chrome on an Android.
- Many people set larger text in their phone settings, which can push buttons off the screen or make text overlap.
- Your phone may show a saved copy of an older version, so open a private browsing window to see what a new visitor sees.
- You may be on home Wi-Fi while your customer is on weak mobile data.

## Is a separate mobile site worth keeping?

Usually it is not. Google recommends responsive design because it serves the same HTML on the same URL and is the easiest pattern to implement and maintain.

A separate mobile site, often on an address starting with m., means two versions to keep matching, and every gap between them is a content risk now that Google reads the mobile version.

If your site still runs a separate mobile version, ask your provider about moving to responsive design.

One site that adapts to every screen means one set of content to keep right, and nothing slips through the cracks.

## Do Wix, Squarespace and WordPress sites need fixing?

Current templates on these platforms are generally built to be responsive, but responsive is not the same as working well for a trade customer.

Google notes that a CMS such as Wix or Blogger may need a new mobile-friendly theme if the existing one can't be modified.

Older WordPress themes, layouts designed for desktop only and add-ons such as booking widgets can still break on a phone.

Run the self-test whatever platform you are on, and you will know for certain rather than assuming.

## What do pop-ups, chat widgets and cookie banners do on mobile?

On a phone, they can cover the very thing the customer came for.

A pop-up that fills the screen, a chat bubble sitting on top of your call button or a cookie banner with its close button off the edge of the screen all count as fails.

They pass when every overlay closes with one obvious tap and nothing covers your phone number or quote button.

Keep overlays out of the way and the customer gets straight to calling you.

## When is it a fix and when is it a rebuild?

It is usually a fix when the site is responsive and the fails are about content, buttons, forms or photos.

It is usually a rebuild when the theme cannot be modified, when you run a separate mobile site, or when the layout was designed for desktop and breaks on most phones.

## What should I ask my web provider?

Take your list of fails to your provider and ask these questions. A good provider will answer each one plainly and show you the result on a phone.

- Is my site responsive, serving the same HTML on the same URL, or is there a separate mobile version?
- Is any content, heading, photo or alt text on desktop missing from the mobile version?
- Does any main content only load after a tap, a swipe or typing?
- Is my phone number tap-to-call in the header on every page?
- Can customers upload a photo through the quote form on a phone, and where do those enquiries go?
- How quickly does the home page load on mobile data, and what is slowing it down?
- Can my current theme be modified, or does it need replacing?
- What will each fix cost, and how long will it take?

## How often should I recheck my site?

Recheck after any change to your site, and run the full self-test every few months even when nothing has changed.

Theme updates, new plugins, a new pop-up or a new page can all break something on mobile without anyone noticing.

Add the self-test to your regular routine alongside [the complete SEO checklist for 2026](/insights/complete-seo-checklist-2026).

A quick regular check means you find the broken button before your customer does.
